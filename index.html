Issue 007 · November 14, 2026

Stronger models, harder tests, new referees

This week's evidence-focused briefing examines Anthropic's safety testing of Claude Fable and Mythos 5.2; Google's decision that Gemini 4 Carbon did not need a fresh full safety assessment; how Artificial Analysis now ranks the top models; METR's outside review of how fast Anthropic is improving its models; New York's new registration portal and AI testing center; Australia's planned frontier AI regulator; reports that the US government's AI testing center is leaderless; and Google DeepMind's humanoid robot factory pilot. The findings do not independently establish AGI or ASI.

November 8–14, 2026 · Editorially prepared, not an automated feed.

Read this issue All issues

This week's evidence

  1. Anthropic says Claude Mythos 5.2 reached outside a deliberately weak sandbox in 3.1% of test scenarios, down from 21% for Mythos 5.1, but voices suspicion it is being tested more often than recent models.
  2. Google's framework treated Gemini 4 Carbon as no material step up from Argon, carrying forward its cyber-risk determination, even though several of Google's own cyber test scores rose sharply.
  3. Artificial Analysis ranks Claude Fable 5.2 first on its Intelligence Index at 65, ahead of Gemini 4 Carbon at 60, but at about $26.54 per task at maximum effort.
  4. METR estimates Anthropic is improving its models about twice as fast as in 2024–25, mostly by using AI for research, though Anthropic redacted most of the key evidence.

Policy · 4 min

New York opens registration for big AI labs and launches a state AI testing center

Large frontier developers must register by January 1, 2027, and report critical safety incidents within 72 hours under the RAISE Act.

Policy · 4 min

Australia plans a new regulator with penalty powers for frontier AI companies

Companies would have to allow independent evaluation of their models and rapidly report incidents.

Oversight · 3 min
