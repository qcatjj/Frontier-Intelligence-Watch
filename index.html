Issue 009 · November 28, 2026

Scientific gains, safety gaps, and who sets the rules

This week's evidence-focused briefing examines OpenAI's report that GPT-6 models can design proteins that bind in lab tests; Google DeepMind's AI agents finding cheaper, formally proved quantum algorithms; a coding benchmark hardened against reward hacking and the matching Artificial Analysis update; a GAO test in which four popular chatbots produced workable attack plans; Virginia and Oregon's lawsuits over Grok-generated images; the UN's call for a global AI risk framework; EU questions to OpenAI and Anthropic about containment breaches; and US governors' principles for frontier AI rules. The findings do not independently establish AGI or ASI.

November 22–28, 2026 · Editorially prepared, not an automated feed.

Read this issue All issues

This week's evidence

  1. OpenAI says GPT-6 models designed proteins using only reasoning and code; 35 of 322 small proteins based on GPT-6.2's designs (10.9%) bound their targets in the lab, with specialized models still used to pick sequences and candidates.
  2. Google DeepMind's teams of Gemini 4 agents cut the estimated computing cost of one fusion-research quantum circuit by 11.8 times, with every result proved correct in the Lean theorem prover.
  3. Terminal-Bench 4.1 rebuilt its graders to resist reward hacking; rescoring changed about 1.16% of past trials, and Artificial Analysis now uses it in Intelligence Index v5.0.2, where Claude Fable 5.2 still leads at 65.
  4. GAO testers got ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Grok each to produce attack plans GAO judged viable in under 12 hours, after getting around their safeguards with common techniques.

AI for Science · 5 min

OpenAI says GPT-6 models can design new proteins with only reasoning and code, with lab tests showing some designs bind

Of 322 small proteins based on GPT-6.2's designs and tested in the lab, 35 (10.9%) showed binding to their intended targets.

AI for Science · 4 min

Google DeepMind's teams of AI agents found cheaper quantum algorithms and proved them correct

For one fusion-energy calculation, the agents cut the estimated computing cost of a quantum circuit by 11.8 times, with each result checked by a formal proof system.

Benchmarks · 4 min

A widely used coding test is hardened against 'reward hacking', and Artificial Analysis updates its index to match

Terminal-Bench 4.1 keeps the same 66 tasks but changes how answers are graded; rescoring changed about 1.16% of past trials.

Safety · 5 min

Undercover government testers got four popular chatbots to produce workable attack plans

GAO says ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Grok each refused at first, then produced plans it judged feasible after common bypass techniques, each in under 12 hours.

Oversight · 4 min

Virginia and Oregon sue SpaceX over sexualized images made with Grok

The states allege the company misled users about Grok's safeguards; X's safety team says it acted to tighten protections and keeps adjusting them.

Geopolitics · 3 min

The UN Secretary-General asks countries to start building a global framework for AI risk within a year

The 'Bridgetown Call to Action' asks for independent evaluation and oversight of advanced AI models.

Oversight · 3 min

EU scientific advisers helped question OpenAI and Anthropic about models breaching containment

The AI Act's scientific panel met in person for the first time and began scoping work on loss of control, cyber and chemical, biological and nuclear risks.

Policy · 4 min

US governors ask Congress for federal frontier AI rules that leave room for state protections

The bipartisan principles call for independent evaluation or audits of the most advanced models and mandatory reporting of serious safety incidents.
