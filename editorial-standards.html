About our research

Editorial standards and corrections

Frontier Intelligence Watch is a free public AI research explainer. Our goal is to make important developments understandable without confusing announcements, evidence, predictions or speculation.

Editorial policy established October 2026. The public archive currently runs through Issue 007.

01. Our mission and independence

We publish accessible summaries of frontier artificial intelligence research, AI safety, autonomy, geopolitics and related data. We aim to help readers understand what has actually been demonstrated, why it matters and what remains uncertain.

Our editorial goal is to follow evidence, not hype. We do not treat an AI company's product launch, a government statement or a viral claim as proof on its own. Coverage does not imply endorsement of any company, government, technology or research conclusion.

Independence: We aim to distinguish reporting and analysis from any sponsorship or commercial relationship. If the publication accepts sponsorships or affiliate compensation in the future, those relationships should be disclosed next to relevant content. This page is a policy statement, not a claim that an independent third-party editorial audit has occurred.

02. Research and sourcing

For important claims, our editorial standard is to link to original material where publicly available: research papers, published evaluations, technical reports, model system cards, public datasets, official disclosures and documented incident reports.

  • Record the original source, publication date, and any relevant update date.
  • Explain the methods and limitations behind a finding, not just the headline.
  • Where practical, cross-check consequential claims against independent evaluations or additional credible sources.
  • Separate research findings from interpretations and predictions.
  • Use attributed quotations sparingly and never invent quotations, figures or source links.
  • Explain research for everyday readers without presenting a simplified analogy as literal scientific proof.

Preprints and preliminary evaluations can be useful, but they are not automatically peer-reviewed or independently replicated. We label their status when material to the conclusion.

03. Evidence labels

These labels describe the type and strength of the available evidence. They do not guarantee that a claim is correct.

INDEPENDENTLY VERIFIED

A finding has credible, independent corroboration that supports the specific claim being made. Verification is limited to the evidence and scope described.

ORIGINAL RESEARCH

A study or evaluation reports results and methods. The study may still be preliminary, contested or unreplicated.

COMPANY / OFFICIAL CLAIM

A developer, institution or government has made a statement that is attributed to them. Their claim is not presented as independent confirmation.

UNVERIFIED / SPECULATIVE

A scenario, forecast, rumor or hypothesis has not been established as fact. We explain what would be needed to verify it.

Our standard for AGI and superintelligence

There is no universally accepted, validated percentage-complete metric for artificial general intelligence or artificial superintelligence. We will not assign invented “progress to ASI” percentages or declare a breakthrough independently verified without credible supporting evidence.

04. Data, graphs and comparisons

Our charts should identify the source, the measurement, its units, the period covered, and when the snapshot was reviewed. Benchmarks, model-release counts, investment totals and estimates are different measures and should not be blended into a single unexplained score.

Where datasets use different definitions, publication dates or geographic coverage, we explain the mismatch. We do not label dated research snapshots as live measurements. Graph bar lengths do not represent probabilities or percentages of superintelligence unless the underlying source actually defines such a quantity.

See our AI Race Tracker for a practical example of sourced comparisons and stated limitations.

05. Corrections and updates

Accuracy matters more than preserving a headline. When we identify a material error, our policy is to correct it promptly after verification and make the change visible to readers.

  • Factual correction: Correct the statement and add a dated correction note describing what changed and why.
  • Clarification: Add context when a statement was technically true but could mislead readers.
  • Updated research: Mark later evidence as an update rather than silently rewriting the original historical record. When a newer issue in this archive supersedes a ranking or availability claim, the original guide keeps its wording and shows a dated update note with a link to the later guide.
  • Retraction: If the central claim is unsupported or fundamentally wrong, preserve an accessible notice explaining the withdrawal instead of quietly deleting the issue.

Our archive is intended to preserve published issues and their correction notices. We do not currently claim a history of published corrections or a third-party verification process.

06. Limitations and use of AI tools

Frontier Intelligence Watch is an independent educational project, not a peer-reviewed scientific journal, academic institution or professional investment or security advisory service. We may use AI tools to help discover research, organize sources and draft plain-English explanations. AI-generated summaries can omit nuance or introduce errors, so publication should depend on checking key claims against cited material.

Automated research discovery feeds may surface unreviewed papers. Such feeds are labeled separately from curated editorial articles. A scheduled publishing workflow does not itself guarantee that a source has been independently verified.

We may not have access to unpublished model weights, classified information, internal company evaluations or all relevant research. Lack of public evidence is not proof that an event is impossible.

07. Questions, tips and correction requests

If you find an error, want to suggest a primary source, or have a question about our methodology, we welcome feedback.

Include the article title or URL, the specific statement in question, and a source supporting the proposed correction. We will review substantiated reports and note material corrections publicly.

Public correction channel: Submit a research or correction issue on GitHub ↗

GitHub requires an account to submit an issue. Please do not include private or sensitive information in a public report. An additional contact option may be added later.
